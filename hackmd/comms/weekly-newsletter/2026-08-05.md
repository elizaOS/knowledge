# ElizaOS Weekly Newsletter (2026-07-29 → 2026-08-04)

## 1) Executive Summary

**A big week for “shipping infrastructure” and clarifying direction.** Here are the headline wins:

1. **`eliza.army` officially launched (skill + leaderboard).** The community contribution pipeline is now much more concrete, with a downloadable contribution skill, a public leaderboard, and CI-backed evidence/attribution expectations to keep quality high.  
2. **Major repo cleanup + CI hardening momentum continued.** The monorepo is being aggressively refactored to remove dead weight and make verification more deterministic—less “it fails on CI sometimes,” more “we can trust the signal.”  
3. **Product focus came into sharper view via Discord.** Community discussion centered on the team building a cross-device consumer app (iPhone, Android, browser, desktop—and even OS-level ambitions), alongside renewed debate about how (or whether) the token fits into that roadmap.

## 2) Development Updates

### Shipping: `eliza.army` contribution skill + leaderboard
The largest ecosystem-facing change this week was the merge of **“feat: launch eliza.army contribution skill and leaderboard”** ([PR #17424](https://github.com/elizaos/eliza/pull/17424)). In practice, this does a few important things:

- Introduces a **bundled “contribute-to-eliza” skill** intended to help contributors finish issues / review PRs with a consistent workflow (claims, validation, evidence, and **exact model disclosure**).
- Adds a Vite site package for **`eliza.army`**, including leaderboard snapshots and work queue visibility.
- Strengthens project hygiene with **evidence and attribution checks** designed to fail closed when information is missing or unverifiable.

If you’ve been waiting for a “what should I work on, and how do I prove it?” pathway—this is the first time it feels end-to-end.

### Repo refactor / cleanup
A very large refactor landed as **“Shaw refactor”** ([PR #17695](https://github.com/elizaos/eliza/pull/17695), merged Aug 4). The PR is enormous in churn (lots of deletions), and while the diff is too broad to summarize line-by-line, the intent is clear: **streamline the repository for the project’s “new direction”** and reduce maintenance burden.

Related (still in flight): **“refactor: clean up repository and harden CI”** ([PR #17585](https://github.com/elizaos/eliza/pull/17585)) remains open and signals more consolidation ahead.

### Deterministic CI & test reliability improvements
A theme across recent merges and issues: **make tests and CI lanes deterministic, especially for contributors and fork PRs.**

- The test-harness isolation work item **“test: isolate agent memory and app-core voice harness state”** (Issue [#17510](https://github.com/elizaos/eliza/issues/17510)) was closed after the merge of **PR #17513** (merged Aug 4). This reduces cross-suite pollution by ensuring harnesses clean up after themselves.
- A notable open CI pain point: fork PR checks getting stuck at `action_required` and causing aggregate gates to time out, wasting runner minutes and showing misleading “red” checks. See **Issue #17551**: [ci: fork-PR aggregate gates poll to timeout on checks held at action_required](https://github.com/elizaos/eliza/issues/17551).

### Localization (i18n) quality: preventing silent drift
An especially detailed open PR, **“fix(i18n): restore the source catalog, purge dead keys, and wire the catalog checker into CI”** ([PR #17606](https://github.com/elizaos/eliza/pull/17606)), highlights a real problem: English (`en.json`) had drifted hundreds of keys behind source because the checker existed—but wasn’t wired into CI. The proposed fix restores missing keys, removes dead keys, and makes translation debt visible without blocking unrelated work.

### Contributor tooling compatibility (GitHub CLI pagination)
Another contributor-experience improvement currently tracked as an issue: **“fix(contribute-skill): support Ubuntu GitHub CLI pagination”** ([Issue #17674](https://github.com/elizaos/eliza/issues/17674)). The gist: Ubuntu’s packaged `gh` version may not support `--slurp`, causing the mandatory inventory step to fail before contribution can even begin.

## 3) Community Spotlight (Discord)

### New faces & onboarding friction (docs needed)
Across **Aug 2–3**, new members introduced themselves and asked how to start contributing—especially around **eliza.army**. Notable moments:

- **kadenschaeferol** joined and said hi in coders.
- **issaarts** (digital artist) offered help with art/animations/banners and asked what the team needs.
- **mochinolabs** repeatedly asked for a clear “start here” path and requested that guidance be shared **publicly in channels** rather than only via DMs—this surfaced a recurring onboarding gap: *we say docs exist, but people can’t quickly find the link.*

Action the community can take now: if you know the best canonical onboarding docs, post them in the relevant channels and pin them.

### Token debate: price, product focus, and trust
On **Aug 4**, discussion in 💬-discussion revolved around the token’s future and how to interpret the current market performance:

- **valleybeyond7991** pushed back on “price down = project dead,” citing bear-market drawdowns across major tokens as context.
- **satsbased** shared updates attributed to Shaw: the team has been working privately and is focused on shipping a **cross-device consumer app** soon. Later, satsbased stated that the **token development phase is effectively done**, and attention is on building working products.
- **dannynor** asked for explicit clarity: is the token abandoned or not?
- **paolin_62616** argued abandoning the token post-migration would be reputationally damaging and unfair to long-term holders—especially those facing steep losses.

This week’s takeaway: the community wants **clear, official messaging** on how token utility aligns (or doesn’t) with product delivery.

### Security reminder: scam attempts around support
A user (**charette6745**) reported a missing deposit for ~2.5 days. Community members warned about fake support links, and **abuzar1553** specifically called out a scammer attempting to impersonate support. If you need help: **do not click random “support ticket” links** and always validate who you’re talking to.
