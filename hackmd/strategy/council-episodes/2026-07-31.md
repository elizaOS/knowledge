## 1) Episode Overview
Episodes reviewed (for strategic context and current priorities):
- **RETRO-2026-06 — Monthly Retro: June 2026**
  - Major step-change toward a managed platform: **Eliza Cloud Apps** (per-tenant isolation), improved **auth (PKCE/allowlists)**, framework modularization (plugin-lifeops split), and an **orchestrator “workbench”** direction.
  - Exposed operational trust gaps: **OAuth 400s**, **ledger mismatches/credit integrity**, and **invite/signup friction**.
- **RETRO-2026-05 — Monthly Retro: May 2026**
  - “Reliability over features” executed via **headless runtime stability**, **@elizaos/vault** for secrets, **ESM-first plugin modernization**, and **safer Jupiter integration**.
  - Surfaced scaling threats: **plugin registry/distribution fragility**, **Telegram message loss edge cases**, **maintainer bus factor**, and **long-lived agent “memory rot” (~90 days)**.

## 2) Key Strategic Themes
- **Reliability becomes the product (not a value statement)**
  - Shift from “we are reliable” to **measured reliability**: auth success rates, message delivery guarantees, crash-free runs, registry uptime, deploy safety, ledger correctness, and long-lived memory integrity.
- **Cloud maturity = operational correctness + onboarding, not just features**
  - With Cloud Apps live, the ecosystem now judges ElizaOS on **SLOs, incident response, deploy safety (blue/green + rollback), and billing/ledger integrity**.
- **Security is now inseparable from reliability**
  - Vault-first, secure defaults, and anti-scam comms are treated as **platform trust primitives**, not add-ons.
- **Modularity vs fragmentation**
  - The architecture is correctly decomposing, but the council highlights the need for **versioning conventions, compatibility matrices, plugin contracts, and registry stability** to avoid “choose your own broken stack.”
- **Longevity and “memory rot” as next-gen reliability**
  - The emerging risk is not crashes—it’s **slow corruption**: retrieval staleness, factual drift, and compounding errors in agents operating for months.
- **DeFAI/MCP strategy pressure**
  - Ecosystem pull toward DeFi execution layers and MCP integrations requires a **clear stance**: standardize boundaries (permissions/contracts), ship one safe reference integration, avoid premature grand abstractions.

## 3) Important Decisions / Insights
- **June 2026 council convergence (RETRO-2026-06):**
  - Treat **auth correctness and ledger integrity** as *core product guarantees*; define and publish Cloud SLOs and operate against error budgets.
  - Prioritize **onboarding funnel fixes** as P0 growth reliability: “Tenant invitation required” and invite errors are treated as stop-the-line issues.
  - Standardize a **capability/permission manifest** across plugins/tools; destructive actions must be policy-gated with audit logs by default.
  - Make DeFAI/MCP an experiment with metrics: **one reference integration** with strong safety rails, not multiple half-finished abstractions.
  - Reduce bus factor via **2+ owners per subsystem**, runbooks, and incident simulations.
- **May 2026 council convergence (RETRO-2026-05):**
  - Registry reliability and Telegram message delivery are elevated from “bugs” to **product-level failures**; reliability must be provable.
  - Establish a **Release Notes + Migration Guides “what changed” contract** as a recurring operational ritual (communication is part of shipping).
  - Launch a **Memory Rot Program**: benchmark first (longevity harness), then mitigation (re-embedding/compaction/provenance/refresh schedules).
  - Vault must become the **default workflow**, minimizing “secret footguns” and making secure deployment easy and visible.

## 4) Community Impact (elizaOS ecosystem)
- **Developers/builders**
  - Highest leverage improvements are now: **install/deploy success**, **registry availability**, **clear upgrade/migration paths**, and **predictable plugin compatibility**—these directly determine whether third-party building compounds or stalls.
- **Operators/teams deploying agents**
  - Cloud Apps changes increase confidence (isolation + hardened auth), but **ledger correctness and deploy safety** determine whether teams trust ElizaOS for production workloads.
- **Plugin authors**
  - ESM-first modernization and modularization are enabling, but **distribution reliability + explicit contracts** decide whether the plugin ecosystem is sustainable.
- **Community trust**
  - Missing release notes, onboarding blockers, and unresolved operational incidents amplify narrative volatility. The council’s position is that **transparent operations + measurable reliability** are the most credible trust repair mechanisms.
- **Long-lived agents / “autonomy over months”**
  - The “memory rot” discussion reframes reliability: a stable runtime isn’t enough if agents degrade silently over time—this affects the credibility of “autonomous agents” as infrastructure.
